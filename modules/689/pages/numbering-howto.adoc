= How this file system works (Schaumburg-Lippe, 1841–1845)
:toc: macro
:toclevels: 2

toc::[]

== 1) The setting — what was Schaumburg-Lippe?
Schaumburg-Lippe was a small German principality in the 1800s. Its government centered on the prince and his central chancellery, with offices that handled daily administration. Two offices matter for your case:
* The **Amt** (district office/court) for local administration and reports.
* The **Rentkammer** (also spelled *Rentcammer*) — the **state treasury/finance department**.

== 2) What the Rentkammer did (and why Krückeberg dealt with it)
*Kept money and books:* ran the **Kammerkasse** (treasury cash office), recorded payments.
*Managed assets:* state lands/forests, leases, sales.
*Handled loans & mortgages:* issued **Obligationen** (bonds/promissory notes) to subjects, secured on land; on repayment it issued a **Quittung** (receipt) and approved **Löschung der Hypothek** (mortgage cancellation).

**Krückeberg’s path (plain English):**
In 1841 he petitioned for a **loan**. The Rentkammer approved and issued an **Obligation** on **12 Jan 1842** for **200 Reichsthaler Courant**, secured on his land. In **1845** he repaid; the Rentkammer receipted it, **approved cancellation of the mortgage**, and **returned the original bond** to him.

== 3) Why there are multiple numbers/stamps on the same sheets
Different desks used **different books** to track the same matter. Each book created its **own** number/mark.

[cols="1a,1a,1a,1a",options="header,autowidth"]
|===
|Mark on page | Belongs to (which book/desk) | What it identifies | Where the number/date live

|*(boxed corner number)* ① ② ③ …
|**Acten-Designation** (the file’s contents list made c. 1841–1842)
|Links the **first page of each internal memo/report** to entries **#1–#10** in the contents list
|On the first page (boxed) and in the **Acten-Designation** table itself

|**N. C. 2696** (examples: 2444, 438 i, …)
|**Rentkammer docket** (treasury’s yearly running docket)
|A **treasury handling/posting** in that year (e.g., recording repayment, issuing receipt, delivery back)
|In the **NC docket register** and written on the handling leaf as “N. C. ####”

|**Nr pr: 1274**
|**Protocol (ledger) book** of the office
|The **line/entry** under which the action was posted in the office’s ledger
|In the **protocol/ledger**; often also jotted once on a leaf as “Nr pr: ####”

|**Nro g. d.**
|**Registry rubric** = *Numero gegeben den* (“No. … given on [date]”)
|Signals the item receives a registry number & date
|Often **not filled on that line**; the number/date are in the registry book or appear elsewhere (e.g., as *Nr pr: …* or alongside an outgoing/incoming stamp)
|===

*Key takeaways*
* **They are parallel systems.** An **NC** number is *not* the same thing as **Nr pr** or the **boxed corner number**.
* It’s normal that **only one** ledger/protocol number (e.g., *Nr pr: 1274*) shows on a single leaf.
* **“Nro g. d.”** is frequently just a tight *rubric* on copies/minutes; the number/date are recorded in the registry book and/or noted elsewhere.

== 4) Acten-Designation (#1–#10): what it is and why #10 looks different
* **Acten-Designation** = the file’s **table of contents** built when the case started (here, 1841–1842).
* Many numbers (rows) can have **multiple entries** because more than one document belongs under that heading.
* The **boxed corner number** appears on the **first page** of each internal memo/report listed as #1–#9.
* **#10** is the **Obligation** (the sealed original bond dated 12 Jan 1842). Originals were often:
** kept in a separate obligations series, and
** **returned to the debtor** upon repayment.
Therefore #10 commonly **does not** carry the file’s boxed corner number and may be **absent** from the case file when you consult it.

**Where your 1845 sheets fit:**  
Sheets marked **NC 2444 / NC 2696** are **treasury follow-ups (Nachträge)** to #10 in 1845: they record repayment, issue the **Quittung**, approve **Löschung der Hypothek**, and deliver the original bond back to Krückeberg. They belong with **#10**, but they were **not** part of the 1841–1842 contents list—hence **no boxed corner number**.

== 5) Other small marks you’ll see (quick glossary)
* **Conc. + initials** (e.g., *Conc. G. W. Fuchs.*) — *Concordat*: “collated; agrees with the original,” attested by the clerk; written on the **office copy**.
* **(L.S.)** — *locus sigilli* (“place of the seal”).
* **F. p.** — in this archive best read as **Fürstliches Placet** (“by princely authorization/approval”), placed just before initials at the end of a text.

== 6) How to label your specific sheets (suggested)
* *Acten-Designation #10 (Obligation, 12 Jan 1842, 200 Reichsthaler Courant)* — original sealed bond; later returned upon repayment; see 1845 follow-ups.
* *NC 2444 (Apr 1845)* — Rentkammer docket follow-up to #10: repayment/liquidation recorded.
* *NC 2696 (5 May 1845)* — Rentkammer docket follow-up to #10: receipt issued; mortgage cancellation approved; delivery of the original bond.

====
*How to read the stamps & numbers on this page*

* **N. C. ####** → Rentkammer **docket number** (treasury handling/posting in that year).
* **Nr pr: ####** → **Protocol/ledger entry** number (the line under which this action was posted in the office’s ledger).
* **(boxed) ①/②/…** → Links this leaf to the **Acten-Designation** contents list (1841–1842).
* **Nro g. d.** → Registry rubric *“Numero gegeben den”* (“No. … given on [date]”); often *not filled on this line*—the number/date are in the registry book or noted elsewhere.
* **Conc. + initials** → *Concordat* (“collated; agrees with the original”), signed by the verifying clerk; appears on office copies.
* **(L.S.)** → *locus sigilli* (place of the seal).
* **F. p.** → **Fürstliches Placet** (“by princely authorization/approval”) just before the closing initials.
====
