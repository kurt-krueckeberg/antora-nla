== Numbering Systems of the Rentkammer in Schaumburg-Lippe (Mid-19th Century)

=== Overview
During the mid-19th century, the Rentkammer (Chamber Treasury) in Schaumburg-Lippe
used parallel numbering systems to track petitions, reports, rescripts, and related acts.
Understanding these systems is crucial for interpreting archival documents, since
each number served a distinct bureaucratic purpose and helps reconstruct the flow of a case file.

=== The “NC \####” Numbers — Chamber Docket / Expedition Numbers

image::nc-xxxx.jpg[link=self]

* *Definition*: Sequential docket numbers assigned by the Rentkammer as items were logged and handled
  during a given year.
* *Usage*: Written by a clerk when the treasury posted or processed an act (petition, repayment, receipt, report).
* *Function*: Marks the chronological order of handling. Multiple NC numbers may occur in a single case file,
  since each discrete act received its own NC.
* *Examples*:  
  - NC 2444, NC 2696 = follow-up entries in 1845 to record repayments or receipts.  
  - NC 4381 = Petition of Krückeberg, October 1841.  
  - NC 4487 = Report of Amt Bückeburg, November 1841.

NOTE: Historians debate the literal expansion of “NC” (e.g., *No. Cammeral*), but
what matters is its function as the *annual docket number of the Rentkammer*.

=== The “Nr pr. \####” Numbers — Protocol / Register Numbers

image::nr-pr.jpg[link=self]

* *Definition*: The entry number in the Rentkammer’s *Protokollbuch* (protocol book), a line-by-line ledger
  recording decisions and actions.
* *Usage*: When an act was officially registered in the protocol, it was assigned a serial number.
  Clerks often wrote that number back onto the document itself.
* *Variants*: Appears as *Nr pr.* or *Num pr.*, with identical meaning.
* *Function*: Represents the *contemporaneous registry number* for the case item,
  distinct from the NC docket number.

=== Related Notations and Abbreviations

* *RC* = shorthand for *Rentkammer*.  
  - Example: "`RC Rescr.`" = *Rentkammer-Rescript*, a formal directive or decision.  
* *RC comment* = Margin note from a Rentkammer clerk or councillor directing the next step (e.g., forward to Amt).  
* *ad NC* = “in reference to NC \####”, linking a later act to its original NC-numbered petition.  

=== Distinction Between the Two Systems

* The *NC number* was simply a running number the Rentkammer gave to each document as it came across their desk.
* The *Nr pr.* was a *protocol register number*, assigned upon entry into the official ledger.  
* Not all documents carry both numbers:  
  - Some petitions show both an NC and a Nr pr.  
  - Others only show NC (if not formally protocolized).  
  - Some case files contain follow-up NC entries but lack additional protocol numbers.

=== Worked Examples

* *Document 1* — Petition of Krückeberg:  
  - NC 4381 (docket number)  
  - Nr pr. 1274 (protocol entry)  
  - Date: 2 Oct 1841, forwarded to Amt Bückeburg.  

* *Document 2* — Report of Amt Bückeburg:

  - Registered under the same protocol reference as Document 1.  

=== Conclusion
The Rentkammer’s dual numbering system — NC docket numbers and Nr pr. protocol entries —
provided both an operational sequence of handling and a permanent ledger reference.
Together they allow today’s researcher to trace the administrative life-cycle of a petition
from initial submission, through treasury handling, to final rescript or repayment closure.
