== Comments on the *NC* and *"Nr Pr"* numbering systems.

=== 1. Why *two* numbering systems?

* **NC number (e.g. NC 4381, NC 4487)**
  → This was essentially the **day-to-day filing number**.
  It told the clerk: *“this piece of paper is document number X in the chamber’s current stack for this year.”*
  It belonged to the *ingress/egress registry* — a running counter of what came in, what went out, with the date.
  Think of it like a **mailroom stamp**.

* **Nr pr. (protocol number)**
  → This was the entry number in the **protocol book** (*Protokollbuch*).
  A protocol book wasn’t just a log. It was the **authoritative diary of decisions, actions, and references**.
  When a rescript was issued, or when a petition was ruled on, the essentials were copied into the protocol book.
  The number “Nr pr. 1274” means: *“If you want the official abstract of this matter, look it up under entry 1274 in this year’s Rentkammer protocol volume.”*

So:

* NC = **document traffic control** (don’t lose the paper).
* Nr pr. = **decision log** (don’t lose the institutional memory).

=== 2. Why wasn’t one number enough?

Because the Rentkammer had **two separate risks** to manage:

* *Physical paper could get lost or misfiled.* (solved by NC stamping).
* *Future clerks might need to know what decision was taken without digging through piles of petitions.* (solved by protocol book entries).

In other words, NC was “we saw this on 2 October and put it in the stack,” while Nr pr. was “this was formally decided as item 1274 in our book of official actions.”

=== 3. What was a protocol book?

Not like “meeting minutes” today, but more like:

* a **ledger of decisions**, written in chronological order,
* containing short summaries of the case and the outcome,
* referenced later whenever someone asked: “What did the chamber decide back in October 1841 about Krückeberg?”

The Rentkammer had to cover its back: decades later, if there was a dispute over whether a loan had been authorized, the **protocol entry** was the proof, not the loose petition in the file.

📌 So, in modern terms:

* **NC number = barcode scan** (every incoming document gets one).
* **Protocol number = case ID in the official case management database** (a record of what the institution decided).
