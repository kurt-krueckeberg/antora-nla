= Administrative Filing Note (rubric)

== Rentkammer filing note (rubric) for #2 (in Acten Designation)

This small note — typically written on a separate slip or at the top of the
first page of a petition — functioned as the formal address and subject
line for filing the document in the Rentkammer’s registry (Kammer-Acten, or
“Cammeral-Acten”).

It was meant for filing:

* The phrasing switches to **genitive** — *“des Colon Krückeberg No 10 …
  unterthänigstes Gesuch um Anlehn dessens pp.”* — which reads like a
**rubric** (“case of Colon Krückeberg…”) rather than the body of a
petition.
* It lacks **date, place, salutation, or signature**—all things you’d
  expect on a submitted petition or forwarding cover.
* The **“pp.”** (perge-perge) and the very terse subject line are classic
  **registratur/docket** shorthand.
* You found it as the **only text on otherwise blank pages**, exactly where
  offices placed an **Actenaufschrift / Rubrum / Umschlagzettel** to label
a file segment.

Workflow (typical in Schaumburg-Lippe ca. 1800):

1. Petitioner submits a **Gesuch** (often via the local **Amt**).
2. The Amt may add a brief forwarding note; then the **Rentkammer’s registratur** creates a **rubric** summarizing
   the case (petitioner, holding number, place, subject).
3. That rubric sits at the front or on a slip attached to the bundle for **organization and indexing**.

So: this slip **summarizes Document 2** (the loan petition) for the Rentkammer’s records. It may have been added at
the Amt or (more likely) at the **Rentkammer** on receipt, but its **purpose is filing/organization**, not
correspondence.

It likely appeared before the petition, but now appears after it when the case file leaves were bound.
