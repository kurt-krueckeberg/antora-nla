= Understanding the Rentcammer Filing System in Schaumburg-Lippe

== What was Schaumburg-Lippe and how did its government work?

Schaumburg-Lippe was a small German principality, a mini-state, in the 1800s. Like other German states of the time,
it had a *Princely court* consisting of the ruler and his central chancellery located in Bückeburg that issued
high-level orders, and it had several administrative offices that ran day-to-day business. Two that matter in this
case are:
[arabic,start=1]
. the *Bückeburg Amt*, the district office and court for local matters, and 
. the *Rentkammer* (also written Rentcammer), the *state treasury and finance department*.

Rentkammer handled:

* *state money* such as taxes, rents, payments in/out of the _Kammerkasse_ (the cash office),

* *state land and forest* leases, sales, and mortgages,

* *loans to subjects* secured on land by issuing *promissory notes*, and later cancelling them when repaid.

== Case File Organization and Document Numbering Systems

There so different numbers and stamps on the pages because *different offices/books* 
were keeping control of the same transaction. These are the most important ones:

=== The Acten-Designation — the case file’s own “table of contents”

When a case file was opened (here, 1841–1842), a clerk wrote a table of contents, a
*contents list* with brief titles.  Each numbered entry in the contents list is composed
of one or more documents; for example, the contents list has these two documents listed
under entry #2:

[cols="1a,5a"]
|===
|2.
|xref:doc-02.adoc#doc-index-2-1[Bericht Amts Bückeburg]

|〃
|xref:doc-02.adoc#doc-index-2-2[Cpt. KRes. an dasselbe]
|===

For each entry, the first document listed will have its Acten Designation entry number
written in the upper right corner of the first page. It will be border by an hand drawn
square.
* That’s why, for entries *#1–#9*, you keep finding a *boxed number* on
the first page of the corresponding memo/letter/report.

[NOTE]
====
*#10* in the case file is *not a memo*; it’s the *Obligation (the sealed
promissory note itself)*. Originals of *promissory notes* were often
*kept in a separate “obligations” series* and, after repayment,
*returned to the debtor*—so they commonly *don’t carry* the file’s boxed
corner number and may not be physically in the case file anymore.
_That’s why #10 has no boxed number in your file._

=== “NC \####” — the Rentkammer’s *docket number* (treasury handling number)

* Think of this as the treasury’s *stamp-pad number for the year* (a
running “docket” sequence).
* It was written *by a Rentkammer clerk* *when* the treasury handled or
posted an item (e.g., recorded repayment, prepared a receipt, arranged
delivery back to the debtor).
* In your 1845 follow-ups you see *NC 2444 / NC 2696* etc. They’re *Nachträge*
(follow-ups) added later to close the loan. These are *later (1845)* treasury
actions (repayment, receipt, return), so they *don’t appear* in the 1841–1842
contents list and *don’t have* boxed corner numbers. 

_(Historians disagree on the exact expansion of “NC”—you’ll see people
gloss it as “No. Cammer(al)” or similar—but what matters is its
*function*: the Rentkammer’s own annual docket number.)_

=== "Nr pr: \####" — the *protocol/ledger entry number*

* Offices kept a *Protokollbuch* (think: a big line-by-line *ledger of
actions*).
* When the action was written into that *book*, the entry got a *serial
number*.
* A clerk often jotted that number back onto the document as *"Nr pr: ++####++*
(read: “Number in the protocol (book): \####”).
* In your file you saw *“Nr pr: 1274”*—that means *this transaction is
recorded under line/entry 1274 in the office’s ledger*. That’s all it
means.

=== “Nro g. d.” — the *registry rubric* (“No. … given on [date]”)

* *Reads:* _Numero gegeben den_ → “No. … given on [date]”.
* *Function:* a *mail/registry template line* indicating the item
*receives a registry number and date*.
* On many petitions/minutes it’s *not filled on that line* (no room is
normal); the *actual number/date live elsewhere*:
* in the *registry book* itself,
* or as a *different notation* on the page (e.g., *Nr pr: 1274*),
* or in a *different system* (e.g., the *NC* stamp).
* So your observation is right: after “Nro” there’s *no space*—because
clerks *weren’t* always writing the number right there. It’s a *rubric*,
not a fill-in blank on these copies.

=== Other page markings

* *“Conc. G. W. Fuchs.”* = _Concordat_ (“collated; agrees with the
original”) + clerk’s name/initials. It appears on the *office copy*, not
the petitioner’s original, and means the clerk *verified the copy
matches* the original.
* *“(L.S.)”* = _locus sigilli_ (“place of the seal”)—marks where the
seal belongs/belonged.
* *“F.p.”* in this archive is best read as *Fürstliches Placet* (“by
princely authorization/approval”)—a chancery approval rubric that sits
just before initials.

=== Summary

* *1841–1842 core file*: contents list (#1–#10). Most items are
*internal letters/reports*, so they show a *boxed corner number* on
their first page. *#10* is the *Obligation* (sealed *promissory
note*)—usually *not* boxed and often *returned* after repayment.
* *1845 follow-ups (NC 2444, NC 2696)*: Rentkammer *treasury actions* to
close the loan—*record repayment*, *issue receipt*, *approve mortgage
cancellation*, *deliver the original back* to Krückeberg. These have *NC
numbers*, *not* boxed corner numbers, because they weren’t part of the
original 1841–42 table of contents.
* *A lone “Nr pr: 1274”* appears where the *ledger entry* was
referenced. It’s normal that it shows on *only one leaf*—you just need
it once to point to the book line.

== How you can label things in your edition (suggested wording)

* *Acten-Designation #10 (Obligation, 12 Jan 1842, 200 Reichsthaler Courant)* — *original sealed *promissory
note*; later returned to debtor upon repayment; see 1845 follow-ups.*
* *NC 2444 (Apr 1845)* — *Rentkammer docket (treasury) follow-up to #10: repayment/liquidation recorded.*
* *NC 2696 (5 May 1845)* — *Rentkammer docket follow-up to #10: receipt issued; mortgage cancellation approved;
delivery of the original *promissory note* to Krückeberg.*
* *Nr pr: 1274* — *Protocol/ledger entry number under which this action was written in the office ledger.*
* *Nro g. d.* — *Registry rubric “No. … given on [date]”; number/date recorded in the registry book (not filled
on this line).*
