.Rescript Explained
****
A Rescript is abbreviated in the Acten Desingation, as " “Rescr.” or “KRes.” (Kammer-Rescript). in the Acten
Designation. It   is a *written official reply or order* issued by a higher authority in response to a petition,
report, or request.

In a legal context: it’s an *authoritative decision communicated in writing*.  In a financial-administrative
context, which is the case here, it’s a *formal directive, decree, or ruling.*

The best translation is "Official Order of the Chamber” or “Decree of the Chamber”.

*Etymology*:

In classical and medieval Latin, *rescriptum*  literally means “something written back” (re-scribere). In
Roman and later European law it *meant a written reply by the ruler* to a petition. German chancery practice
inherited the term directly from Latin, and it shows up in 18th–19th century files as Rescriptum or abbreviated
Rescr.  Rescript is simply the shortened form of the Latin Rescriptum, borrowed into German (Reskript) and then
into English.
****

.Carifications
****
Item *#10* in the case file is *not a memo*; it’s the *Obligation (the sealed promissory note itself)*. Originals
of *promissory notes* were often *kept in a separate “obligations” series* and, after repayment, *returned to the
debtor*—so they commonly *don’t carry* the file’s boxed corner number and may not be physically in the case file
anymore.  _That’s why #10 has no boxed number in the case file._

In item *#7* the *Cpt. K. Res. aus Amt Bückeburg*, *aus* is incorrect. The actual document itself has *an* not
*aus*: *Concept an das Amt Bückeburg*, bears no *pr. in Cam.* (incoming-presented) mark, and carries the Kammer
closing *Fürstl: p.*. *aus* is retained in the transliteration for accuracy, but rendered as *to the Amt Bückeburg*
in the English translation, noting the likely clerical slip.
****

=== Comments and Insights

The first column *"No"* is a case item or grouping number for a cluster of related documents. Each number groups together
the related petitions, reports, and official orders concerning a particular stage in the case, in which one aspect
of the overall decision making process is under consideration. The description of the overall case, what the Rentcammer is
trying to ultimately decided, is given by entry 1: the petition of Colon Krückeberg of No. 10 Berenbusch for a
loan of 200 Reichsthaler.

In entry 3's *"Acten Designation (right)"* "gegen" in "gegen Verpfändung seiner sämmtlichen Ländereien" does not mean
"against", its typically meaning, but rather "on condition of". The Rentcammer was willing to consider a loan but only
against the pledge of his entire holding. The Chamber’s willingness to approve a loan was conditional upon his pledging
all of his landholdings as collateral.
