This is procedurally central:

* The Rentkammer *does not decide on the petition itself*.

Crucially, this inquiry concerns:

== 3. Identity and legal status of the petitioner

=== a. Social and legal position

The petitioner identifies himself as:

* *"`Schneider Krückenberg in Evesen`"*
* living *as an Einlieger* at *Wilharm Nr. 11 in Evesen*.

This establishes him as:

* landless,
* non-hereditary,
* without independent real property.

However, this does *not* place him outside the intended pool of Colon
candidates. In Schaumburg-Lippe practice, *craftsmen and tradesmen were
entirely eligible* for Colon status, provided they could sustain a Haus-
und Gartenstätte.

'''''

=== b. Intended status change

The petition seeks a *change in tenure*, not in profession:

* from *Einlieger* → *Colon (Neubauer)*,
* from dependent lodging → independent house-and-garden holding.

No claim is made, implicitly or explicitly, to agricultural identity.

'''''

== 4. Description and prior status of the requested land

=== a. Origin and administrative status

The petitioner describes a site:

* *"`vor Bärenbuscht`"*
* already *inspected by the Forstamt* in connection with an earlier,
unsuccessful applicant.

This shows:

* the land originates from *Forstgrund*,
* it has already passed *preliminary administrative screening* for
colonization,
* it is part of a *managed state colonization policy*, not an ad-hoc
request.

This aligns exactly with the later file wording:

____
_"`vor Forstgrunde`"_
____

'''''

=== b. Physical condition of the site

He emphasizes that the site is:

* uneven,
* requires filling and leveling,
* demands "`viele Mühe.`"

This description is *deliberate and strategic*, not incidental. It
functions to:

* justify a *lower purchase or ground price*, and
* pre-empt objections based on inferior land quality.

The argument is economic, not agrarian.

'''''

== 5. Occupational logic and site suitability

A key passage states:

____
"`… weil er nicht zu weit von Lauten entfernt ist, worauf ich wegen
meiner Schneider Profession besonders Bedacht nehmen muß.`"
____

This is central to understanding the petition correctly:

* "`Lauten`" refers to *people / clientele*, not a locality.
* The petitioner explicitly ties site suitability to *craft-based
income*.
* He is not seeking arable land, but *accessibility*.

This is *fully consistent with Colon status*, which in Schaumburg-Lippe
was *tenurial*, not occupational.

'''''

== 6. Legal formulation of the request

The request is narrowly and precisely framed:

____
*"`… mir jenen Platz zum Hausbau gnädigst gegen ein Billiges zu
überlassen.`"*
____

He asks for:

* *Überlassung* (allocation),
* *zum Hausbau*,
* at a *reasonable price*.

He does *not* request:

* farmland,
* hereditary estate rights,
* tax exemptions,
* or free land.

The scope of the request matches exactly what later becomes:

____
*Colonie-bau (Haus und Garten)*.
____

'''''

* *Signed:* 29 September 1808
* *Received:* 30 September 1808

= Sources for Explanation of Terms in Schaumburg=Lippe Rentkammer

Here are *independent, citable sources* you can use to pin down

== 1) Deutsches Rechtswörterbuch (DRW) — lexicographic "`baseline`" definitions

These are _not Schaumburg-Lippe-specific_, but they’re a very strong
independent control because they compile legal/administrative usage
across German territories with dated attestations.

* *Einlieger (DRW)* — defined (with citations) as _free persons who
settle in a village without taking an "`unterthäniges Gut`" and without
binding themselves to personal subjection; such persons are called
Schutzuntertanen or Einlieger_ (the DRW quotes an 18th-century
ordinance-style formulation).
(https://drw.hadw-bw.de/drw-cgi/zeige?index=lemmata&term=einlieger&utm_source=chatgpt.com[drw.hadw-bw.de])
* *Neubauer (DRW)* — defined succinctly as _"`der sich als Bauer neu
ansiedelt`"_ (someone who settles newly as a farmer/bauer).
(https://drw-www.adw.uni-heidelberg.de/drw-cgi/zeige?index=lemmata&term=neubauer&utm_source=chatgpt.com[drw-www.adw.uni-heidelberg.de])

Why this helps your case file: your cover label explicitly says
*"`Neubauer … Krückeberg`"* and the petition describes moving from
*Einlieger* status toward establishing his own household/building site.
DRW gives you *neutral definitions* you can cite without relying on any
one regional historian.

== 2) J. Arndt (1992) — scholarly regional social/agrarian context (Lippe, with explicit Schaumburg-Lippe linkage)

A very usable (and quotable-by-paraphrase) discussion is available in
the LWL-hosted PDF of Arndt’s work.

=== Einlieger: social position / village membership (useful for interpreting "`Einlieger bei Wilharm No. 11`")

Arndt describes *Einlieger* as _not belonging to the village community_,
but being allowed (depending on arrangements) limited use-rights such as
putting 1–2 head of cattle on the common land; they appear socially as
part of a patron’s "`Gefolge,`" etc.
(https://www.lwl.org/westfaelische-geschichte/txt/normal/txt262.pdf[lwl.org])
(see the passage around *P61, L2679–L2684*)

=== Neubauern: state policy of creating new small holdings (Binnenkolonisation)

Arndt explicitly frames "`Neubauern`" in the context of *land policy*:
the government tried to counter land scarcity by *creating new
Bauernstellen*, and "`Neubauern`" were drawn into *grund- und
leibherrliche Abhängigkeit* (ground- and personal-lordship dependency),
with the new settlements tending to be *small farms/holdings*.
(https://www.lwl.org/westfaelische-geschichte/txt/normal/txt262.pdf[lwl.org])
(see *P62, L2719–L2725*)

=== Why this is especially relevant to you

Arndt’s bibliography in that same section explicitly points to *Georg
Funke, "`Über die Verhältnisse der Einlieger in den Fürstentümern
Lippe-Detmold und Schaumburg-Lippe`" (1848)* as a targeted study of
_Einlieger_ for *both principalities*.
(https://www.lwl.org/westfaelische-geschichte/txt/normal/txt262.pdf[lwl.org])
(see *P61, L2698–L2704*)

So even though Arndt’s monograph is centered on Lippe, it *directly
bridges* into *Schaumburg-Lippe* for the _Einlieger_ topic via Funke.
