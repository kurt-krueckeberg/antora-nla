= Document 11: Draft purchase deed for this Krückeberg, No. 18, in Berenbusch, concerning a building site for house construction and garden
:page-role: wide

[.narrow]
== Overview

=== What this document is

A draft purchase instrument issued by the Rentkammer for Jobst Heinrich
Krückeberg, No. 18 (Berenbusch), functioning at this stage as the legally
effective deed for a hereditary tenancy holding, in place of a formal
Kaufbrief (*statt Kaufbriefs*).

=== What it records (the essential facts)

* *Object of purchase:* a *house-and-garden plot* in the *Baumer Forst*.
* *Legal basis:* the Rentkammer previously *approved* that this plot
could be *sold to him*.
* *Valuation/price rule:* set at *6 Mariengroschen per Quadrat-Ruthe*
(per square rod), based on expert assessment.
* *Survey:* the plot was *measured by the sworn surveyor Stille* in
Bückeburg.
* *Size:* *102 Quadrat-Ruthen + 51 Quadrat-Fuß*.
* *Payment:* the calculated purchase price—*17 Reichsthaler 3
Mariengroschen*—has been *paid into the Kammer-Casse*.

=== What this purchase instrument does legally

==== 1. What “ownership (Eigenthum)” means here

The instrument grants **Eigenthum (ownership) in the contemporary cameral
sense**, not modern freehold ownership. This means that:

* Krückeberg acquires a **heritable and legally protected right to the
holding** (house plot and garden),
* the holding may pass to his **heirs**, and
* his rights cannot be arbitrarily revoked.

However, the land **remains within the Rentkammer’s regulated land
system**. Ownership is **qualified**, not absolute.

==== 2. What the “title guarantee” actually guarantees

When the document states that Krückeberg and his heirs are to be:

> “protected at all times and against everyone,”

this guarantees that:

* no third party may lawfully dispossess him,
* his holding is secure against competing claims,
* and his rights are enforceable in law.

It **does not** mean:

* freedom from state oversight,
* freedom from dues,
* or freedom to dispose of the land at will.

==== 3. What his ongoing obligations are

The holding is **burdened with annual cameral dues (Prästanden)** and
other obligations tied to the land.

* These dues are an **inherent condition** of the holding.
* Even though the detailed list is struck through in this draft, the
  **obligation itself remains binding**.
* Payment of these dues is what keeps the holding in good standing.

Failure to meet these obligations could ultimately jeopardize the holding.

==== 4. How this differs from modern U.S. land ownership

For a modern reader, the key difference is this:

* In modern U.S. law, a landowner typically has **unrestricted freehold title**.
* In this case, Krückeberg holds a **heritable, state-regulated tenancy holding**:

  * he may live on it,
  * build and maintain a house,
  * pass it to his heirs,
  * and enjoy strong legal protection,
  * **but** only while fulfilling ongoing obligations and obtaining approval for major legal acts (sale, mortgage).

=== How it fits in the case workflow

This document is the *ownership step* after:

[arabic]
. approval,
. survey,
. payment, and before/alongside issuance of the *Meyerbrief*, which is
the more comprehensive tenure instrument (conditions, ongoing dues,
etc.). The document itself confirms this staging by saying it is issued
*"`instead of`"* a full Kaufbrief.

.Translation

Draft purchase deed issued in the name of the Rent Chamber
for Jobst Heinrich Krückeberg,
No. 18 at Berenbusch,
concerning a plot of land for the construction of a house
and a garden in the Baumer Forest.

Dispatched by Schwarze.

Whereas, upon the application of Jobst Heinrich Krückeberg,
No. 18 at Berenbusch, the Princely Rent Chamber has approved
that a plot of land for the purpose of building a house and
establishing a garden in the Baumer Forest shall be conveyed
to him by purchase, on the condition that he pay the value
assessed by the appointed experts, at the rate of six
Mariengroschen per square rod; and whereas the said plot,
measuring 102 square rods and 51 square feet, has been
surveyed by the sworn land surveyor Stille at Bückeburg; and
whereas the determined purchase price, calculated according
to the above rates, amounting to seventeen Reichsthaler and
three Mariengroschen, has been paid into the Princely Chamber
Treasury; and whereas the said Krückeberg is obligated to
pay the annual cameral dues assessed for this plot in due
time and in proper manner;

the aforementioned new settler Krückeberg, No. 18 at
Berenbusch, and his heirs are hereby granted ownership of
this plot purchased by him, and assurance is given that he
and his heirs shall be protected in their possession thereof
at all times and against all persons.

In witness whereof, this instrument, in lieu of a formal
purchase deed, has been executed under the printed seal of
the Princely Chamber and with the customary signatures.

Bückeburg, 10 January 1810.

Princely Schaumburg-Lippe
Rent Chamber,
by order.

von Danckwertz     M. Kaas
Sandesbur. Windt
