== Overview and Summary

Document 2 is the report of the administrative offices of Bückeburg and Arensburg, requested by the Rentkammer
after its initial review of Jobst Heinrich Krückeberg’s petition to purchase a tract of land from the princely
authorities at a reasonable price, on the grounds that the land was uneven and would require substantial
earth-moving before a house could be built on it.

The number *2* is visible in the upper right corner of the document. It appears
bordered by an hand-drawn square. Document 2 is contained on two images. 

The Rentkammer's response to the report is written vertically along the
left side of the first page. The third image below contains this text
rotated so it can be read. The Rentkammer's response ends with an asterisk
resembling a double dagger, and the text referenced by the double dagger is at
the bottom of the first page.

=== Translation

[NOTE]
====
An *Ackervogt* was a locally appointed agricultural overseer
or field bailiff responsible for supervising arable land and
farming practices. The office signaled official trust and
local respectability rather than full-time government
employment. Its mention here suggests that Jobst Heinrich
Krückeberg’s father likely had some means, making it
plausible that Jobst Heinrich inherited a modest sum and
could reasonably finance the construction of his own house.
====

.Translation of Document 2
[literal.bordered,subs="verbatim,quotes"]
....
N.C. 287.                         received 27 Oct. 1808.

To the
Princely Chamber
Report of the Offices of Bückeburg
and Arensburg
re.
New settlement.

The tenant Jobst Henr[ich] Krückeberg is, as is also his
wife, a native of Evesen. His father later became Ackervogt
at Arensburg, from whom he may well have inherited so much
that he, as he assures, will at most require no more
than 200 Reichsthaler, after collecting the outstanding
payments owed to him for his work [as a tailor], in order to
build a house on a property near Berenbusch, to be financed
through a loan requiring official approval.

He is by profession a tailor, whose work is found in and
around Berenbusch, and he therefore considers the purchase
of a property elsewhere that is being sold due to the
owner’s debts to be far less advisable for himself than
building his own house in Berenbusch.

He and his wife are known to us to be of good reputation,
and we hereby attest to their diligence and orderly conduct.
A copy of the petition is enclosed.

Bückeburg, the 21st
of October 1808.
Habicht Neussel
....
