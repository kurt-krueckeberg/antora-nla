# **What Multiple NC Numbers Mean**

### **1) NC does not mean “the case number” the way we mean “case number” today.**

**NC (“N. Cam.” or “N. Causa”) is the registry number assigned by the Rentkammer *at the moment it receives or processes a document.*
Each **entry** in the Rentkammer received **its own NC number.**

So:

* One **petition** = one NC
* One **Amt report** = another NC
* One **Actum / protocol return** = another NC
* One **resolution / order** = another NC

They all belong to the **same matter**, but each **step of the process** is assigned **its own NC number** when it arrives in the Rentkammer.

### So:

**NC does not identify the case.
NC identifies each stage of processing inside the Rentkammer.**

---

# **2) Why these NC numbers appear in the same folder**

Because **after the case was completed**, the Rentkammer clerk **pulled all NC entries that referred to the same subject** (Krückeberg selling his Stätte and emigrating) and **physically filed them together.**

That is what created your **case file**.

So your file contains **all NC numbers that correspond to the same legal matter**.

---

**This is normal.**
This is exactly how Rentkammer files look in Schaumburg-Lippe, Preußen, Hessen, Braunschweig, etc., in the 18th–19th c.

# **4) So what identifies the *case*, if NC does not?**

**The subject line (Betreff) does.**

For example:

```
Betr: Gesuch des Colons Krückeberg Nr. 10 in Berenbusch
     um Erlaubniß zum Verkaufe seiner Stätte.
```

**This** is the true “case identifier.”

This is why archivists use **the subject**, not **NC**, for cataloguing.
